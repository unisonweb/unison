`unison-src/transcripts-manual/jit-benchmarks.md` on the optimized build
(`.stack-work-opt`), `UNISON_JIT=on`, with `UNISON_WIDE=off` and then
`UNISON_WIDE=on`, one run each, the machine otherwise idle (macOS arm64,
commit 72f397640 plus this file). Times are the transcript's own, in
milliseconds; the ratio is off over wide, so above 1 is a gain.

What the rows say:

- The rows the convention is for gain: a small constructor returned from a
  call and matched by the caller no longer allocates. `Ok or Err from a step
  function` 2.5x, `Apply a function argument` 4.5x, `Cons list: head` 1.8x,
  `Binary tree: 1000 lookups` 1.3x (an `Optional` per lookup).
- Rows that return no constructor pay for the wider return: `Calls across
  definitions: Collatz` 0.45x and `callee first reached once the caller is
  hot` 0.55x are the worst, `fib 20` 0.71x, `Cons list: map` 0.74x,
  `murmurHashUntyped` 0.73x, `filterMap` 0.81x, the state tuple 0.85x. Every
  non-tail call now returns three (word, pointer) pairs where it returned
  one, and stores three slots on return. The Collatz row is calls through
  cells between modules, so the cost is in the uniform return through the
  stack and the caller's reload, not in register pressure; to be measured
  on its IR next, before the convention is tuned (step 7 of
  delayed-data-construction.local.md).
- The bulk of the suite (texts, bytes, floats, arrays, records, requests)
  is within 5% either way; the handler rows are slightly faster.
- Small rows (under 0.1 ms) are near the timer's resolution and their
  ratios are rough.
