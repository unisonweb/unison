# 2026-10-31: the wide calling convention at K = 1 against K = 2

`unison-src/transcripts-manual/jit-benchmarks.md` and
`unison-src/transcripts-manual/jit-suite.md` on two optimized builds
(`.stack-work-opt`) of commit 9d8ba5dd0, one with `wideK = 2` (as merged)
and one with `wideK = 1`, under `UNISON_JIT=on`, interleaved, best of
three runs of the benchmark transcript and best of two of the library
suite, macOS arm64, the machine otherwise idle. Times are the transcripts'
own, in milliseconds; the ratio is K = 2 over K = 1, so above 1 is a gain
for K = 1. The question (Paul, 2026-10-31): K = 2 was chosen to start,
"the census decides whether K = 1 would do", and the census was never
read on this point. It said that in library code one-field constructors
(`Some`, `Left`, `Right`) are returned about six times as often as
two-field ones (pairs), and the one pair-in-flight row had lost under the
convention (0.71x, `2026-10-28-wide-jit.md`).

At K = 1 a wide variable is two slots instead of three, a worker returns
five words instead of seven (status and two (word, pointer) pairs), and a
pair is boxed where it is built.

## What it showed

- The rows that return `Optional` gain: `map` 1.31x, `filterMap` 1.25x,
  tree lookups 1.07x, the hash of `Some (i, "x")` 1.08x. In the library
  suite most rows are 3% to 10% faster (`Thirty match` 1.10x, the
  `Remote.Ref` row 1.06x, `fib2` and `fib3` 1.06x, `Set.fromList` and
  `NatMap.fromList` 1.05x), consistently across the runs.
- The rows with a pair in flight lose, as the width predicts: the state
  tuple through a non-tail call 0.85x (the pair is boxed where it was two
  slots), the two `Store` handler rows 0.95x and 0.97x, consistent across
  three runs, and `Multimap.fromList` 0.91x. The static product-return
  worker (optimization-ideas.md) is the planned answer for a known callee
  returning a pair to a loop, with no K involved.
- Everything else is within 2%.
- K = 1 is committed. The correctness runs at K = 1: the test transcript
  under the interpreter, `on` and `eager` with identical output, and the
  `serial-test` and `random-deserial` transcripts in their three modes.
