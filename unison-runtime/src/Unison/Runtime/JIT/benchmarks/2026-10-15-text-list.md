# Texts and lists as closure constructors (2026-10-15)

A text was `Foreign (WrapText rope)` and a list `Foreign (WrapSeq deque)`: two closures of
two words each before the rope or deque, allocated for every result, and two loads and two
checks (`Foreign`'s info pointer, since `Foreign` has tag 7, then the wrapper's tag and info)
on every primitive that reaches the rope. Now a text is `TextC rope` (`GText`) and a list
`ListC deque` (`GList`): one object of two words, one load, and among the first six
constructors of `GClosure` the check is the pointer tag alone.

The order decided with Paul: `ListC` 1, `TextC` 2, `Enum` 3, `Data1` 4, `Data2` 5, `Data3` 6,
and `Data4` moves to tag 7 (the sixth tag then went to `Data5` instead, measured below). The order among the six is immaterial (each has its own tag; a
case is a jump through the tag bits), so the only question was which six. The suite can't
rank `Data3` against `Data4` (nothing in it matches a three-field constructor, so the
dispatch counts of [2026-10-15-constructor-order.md](2026-10-15-constructor-order.md) say
zero for `Data3`), and the smaller shape stayed in by reasoning: a wider match pushes more
fields and absorbs the one extra info-table load better. The tree rows below are the cost of
that choice, since a tree node has four fields.

Nothing on the wire changed: texts and lists are their own `BLit` cases of the ANF `Value`,
and code holds `MLit (MT t)`. `Universal.compare` is unchanged: the new kinds keep
`closureNum` 3, two texts or two lists compare as `compareForeign` did, and two different
builtin kinds under `Any` (a text against bytes) raise as before, through explicit cases in
`compareClosure` and `Eq Closure` rather than the `closureNum` fallback.

What changed: the `BuiltinForeign` class is typed through `Closure` (`wrapBuiltin :: f ->
Closure`), its `Text` and `Deque Val` instances build the new constructors and the other 41
go through `Foreign`; `Eq Closure`, `compareClosure`, `closureNum`, `closureTermRefs`, the
reflection and reification in `Machine.hs`, `Decompile.hs`, the JSON writer and the list
convention in `Function.hs`, the pool's text literal; `Layouts` probes `lText` and `lList`
and hands their info pointers and tags to `unison_jit_list_init` and `unison_jit_text_init`
(the bytes kind keeps the two-level form: `RopeKind.direct`); `deque_of` and `rope_of` are
one tag compare and one load; the C builders of text and list results write two words;
the hash and comparison walkers accept the new info pointers. The generator needed no
change: its six `taggedObject "foreign"` sites are arrays, pointers and tickets, and every
text and list operation reaches the rope or deque through the C helpers.
`jit-tests.md` gained a stanza "Texts and lists as closure constructors"; the three modes
agree.

## `Data5` against `Data3` for the sixth tag

Paul's observation: base's `Map` node is `Bin size key value left right`, five fields, and
under the JIT it is the real constructor, since the Haskell `Map` replacement is
interpreter-only (`mapFamily` in `Stack.hs`). So every native map operation matched a
`Data5` at tag 7 (since `PAp`'s move on 2026-10-15; before that `Data5` held tag 6), while
`Data3` held a tag that nothing in either suite exercises. Measured by swapping the two in
the declaration (`dataOk` in `Layout.hs` follows): `jit-replacements.md`, which times
base's own `Map`, `Set` and `List` code, native, twice; then the suite once. ms per row,
`Data3` tagged against `Data5` tagged, second round (the first agrees within a point or
two):

The Nat-keyed inserts and lookups, where the match is the largest share of the work, gain
8 to 13% in this pair; the text-keyed and the union, intersect, toList and `==` rows 0 to
4%; the `List.sort`, JSON and `Bytes.read` rows, which build no maps, are unchanged. In the
suite nothing moves (tree rows 0.103 vs 0.104 and 0.0391 vs 0.0391, the text, list and
records rows within 1%): no row matches a three-field constructor. `Data5` keeps the sixth
tag.

The pair overstates the tag, though. The chain below runs the same transcript on the day's
four binaries, and the morning's second binary (`PAp` moved to 7, so `Data5` at tag 7 with
everything else as now, give or take texts and lists, which the Nat-keyed rows don't touch)
is within 2 to 5% of the final one: insert 10 keys 42 → 40 ns, insert 1000 keys 117 → 114,
lookup in 100000 keys 123 → 117. The `Data3`-tagged binary was slower than both on the same
rows (44, 129, 127 ns) for a reason the tag doesn't explain, the kind of placement effect
the constructor-order file records for the hash and tree rows. So the sixth tag is worth
2 to 5% to the maps, and the choice costs nothing; the decision stands.
